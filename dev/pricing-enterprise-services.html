When Infrastructure Becomes a Business Risk.

Enterprise infrastructure failures are rarely caused by missing technology. They occur when operational responsibility is fragmented, escalation paths are unclear, and accountability is shared across vendors with no single owner.

In organizations where infrastructure availability, security posture, and operational clarity directly affect business continuity, compliance, and executive accountability, this fragmentation becomes a material business risk—not an IT inconvenience.
Crafthost Enterprise Services exist for these environments.

This service is designed for enterprises that require a single accountable operator for mission-critical infrastructure, operating under contractual SLAs, audit-ready processes, and executive-level governance.

What Enterprise Services Mean at Crafthost.

Enterprise Services shift operational responsibility from fragmented internal teams and multiple vendors to one accountable operator.

Crafthost does not act as a reseller, MSP, or best-effort support provider.We operate as an externalized infrastructure operations function, contractually accountable for outcomes under clearly defined service assurance models.

This model is adopted by organizations that:

  • Cannot tolerate ambiguous responsibility boundaries
  • Operate under regulatory, audit, or contractual pressure
  • Require predictable outcomes rather than reactive support

The Real Cost of Infrastructure Failure.

The true cost of infrastructure failure is rarely the monthly service fee.

It is typically measured in:

  • Revenue loss during outages or degraded performance
  • Regulatory penalties, audit findings, or compliance failures
  • Security exposure and incident response fallout
  • Missed SLAs and contractual disputes
  • Internal team burnout and operational distraction

Enterprise Services exist to reduce these risks by transferring operational ownership to a single accountable party.

Pricing Reflects Risk & Responsibility.

Enterprise Services pricing is non-linear by design.

Pricing is determined by who carries the operational risk, who responds to incidents, and who is accountable for outcomes—not by VM counts, storage volumes, or raw infrastructure usage.

Pricing Is Shaped by Six Factors:

Operational responsibility and governance scope.

Service level objectives and response commitments.

Regulatory and compliance requirements.

Architectural complexity and system topology.

Geographic distribution and performance targets.

Business impact associated with service disruption.
