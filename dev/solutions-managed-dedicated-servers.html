What Managed Dedicated Servers Mean at Crafthost.

At Crafthost, managed dedicated servers represent an operational ownership model, not a hosting configuration.

Responsibility extends across the physical and system layers, including continuous monitoring, controlled maintenance, security governance, incident ownership, and reliability engineering under defined SLAs. Infrastructure operations are externalized while architectural authority and strategic control remain internal.

This solution is designed for organizations that:

  • Require deterministic, single-tenant performance
  • Need physical isolation for security or regulatory reasons
  • Cannot accept virtualization overhead or noisy neighbors
  • Expect senior engineering accountability during incidents
  • Require clear ownership across the hardware lifecycle

Built for Determinism, Not Elastic Abstraction.

In environments where outcomes depend on consistency, elastic abstraction introduces risk rather than value.

Managed Dedicated Servers prioritize:

  • Stable CPU and memory behavior
  • Predictable I/O throughput and latency
  • Isolated failure domains
  • Controlled capacity planning
  • Disciplined, auditable change management

This operating model removes uncertainty created by shared environments and layered virtualization.

Managed Dedicated Servers — FAQ.

How are managed dedicated servers different from unmanaged bare metal?

Managed Dedicated Servers include full operational ownership by Crafthost. Unmanaged bare metal provides hardware access only.

Do customers retain control over hardware choices?

Yes. Customers retain architectural and strategic control. Crafthost assumes operational responsibility.

Is this suitable for regulated industries?

Yes. Physical isolation and governed operations support compliance-aligned environments.

Can you manage existing dedicated servers?

Yes. Existing environments can be onboarded following assessment and operational alignment.

Is pricing fixed?

No. Pricing is assessment-based and reflects hardware complexity, risk, and SLA depth.
