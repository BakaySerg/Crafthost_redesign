Engineering-Level Documentation for Real-World Infrastructure Operations.

Crafthost Technical Guides are deep, implementation-focused documents written for engineers and architects who are responsible for designing, operating, and stabilizing production systems.

These are not marketing assets.
They are operational references.

Each guide is designed to help teams build, validate, and maintain infrastructure that must perform correctly under load, failure, and regulatory pressure.

What These Guides Are — and Are Not.

What They Are:
  • Practical engineering documentation
  • Architecture-aware operational guidance
  • Failure-mode–driven explanations
  • Production-first assumptions
  • Written by and for infrastructure professionals
What They Are Not:
  • Introductory tutorials
  • Vendor feature walkthroughs
  • Generic “how-to” articles
  • Simplified cloud onboarding content
  • Opinion pieces without operational grounding

These guides assume you already understand the basics — and need answers that hold up in production.

Core Areas Covered.

Infrastructure Architecture & Design

How to design systems that remain stable during change and failure.
Topics include:

  • Fault domain isolation
  • Blast radius containment
  • Capacity reservation models
  • Tier IV and Tier V engineering principles
  • Multi-region and multi-zone design trade-offs

Network Engineering & Traffic Control

Ensuring deterministic performance and predictable latency.
Covers:

  • East–west vs north–south traffic isolation
  • Oversubscription avoidance
  • Deterministic routing paths
  • Multi-carrier resilience
  • Convergence behavior during link failure

Availability, Uptime & Failure Handling

Designing systems that fail gracefully — or not at all.
Includes:

  • SLA vs architectural availability
  • Concurrent maintainability
  • Planned maintenance without impact
  • Elimination of hidden single points of failure
  • Failure injection and validation practices

Security Architecture & Enforcement

Security as an architectural constraint, not an add-on.
Topics:

  • Infrastructure-level security controls
  • Network segmentation and isolation
  • DDoS mitigation foundations
  • Zero-trust networking considerations
  • Security boundaries between infrastructure and workloads

Compliance & Regulated Environments

Operating infrastructure that survives audits, not just incidents.
Covers:

  • Data residency enforcement
  • Jurisdictional isolation
  • Infrastructure evidence and documentation
  • Compliance-aware architecture design
  • Supporting finance, healthcare, government, and telecom workloads

Performance Engineering & Capacity Planning

Avoiding degradation before it happens.
Includes:

  • Deterministic performance models
  • Load behavior under sustained demand
  • Capacity forecasting
  • Avoiding burst-induced instability
  • Performance testing that reflects production reality

Operations, Monitoring & Incident Response

How real operations differ from dashboards.
Explains:

  • Signal vs noise in monitoring
  • Alert fatigue prevention
  • Root cause isolation
  • Incident ownership boundaries
  • Post-incident validation and remediation

How These Guides Are Written.

Each guide follows a consistent framework:

Context & Problem Statement

Architectural Assumptions

Common Failure Patterns

Correct Design & Configuration

Operational Considerations

Validation & Testing Methods

What This Approach Avoids

No guide ends with “it depends” without defining what it depends on.

Who These Guides Are For & Who Who Should Not Use These Guides.

Who These Guides Are For:
  • Infrastructure engineers
  • Platform and SRE teams
  • Network and security architects
  • Senior DevOps professionals
  • Technical leaders accountable for uptime
Who Should Not Use These Guides:
  • Beginners learning basic cloud concepts
  • Non-production environments
  • Teams without operational ownership
  • Feature comparison readers

These documents are intentionally dense and precise.

How Teams Use These Guides.

Organizations use these technical guides to:

  • Validate architecture designs
  • Train senior engineers
  • Standardize operational practices
  • Prepare for audits and compliance reviews
  • Reduce mean time to recovery (MTTR)
  • Prevent recurring failure patterns

They are often referenced during incidents, not just during planning.

Related Resources.

  • Strategic decision frameworks
  • Operational documentation
  • Architecture & pricing clarity

Most production failures are not caused by missing tools. They are caused by incorrect assumptions carried into production.

These technical guides exist to surface those assumptions — before they turn into outages, incidents, or regulatory failures.
